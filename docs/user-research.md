# StyleIQ User Research Plan

**Status:** Planned research — no interviews or survey results are claimed in this document.

## Research objective

Understand how online fashion shoppers evaluate appearance, size, fit, and product confidence before purchasing clothing. Determine whether a measurement avatar, photo avatar, or simpler fit guide addresses a meaningful unmet need.

## Research questions

1. Tell me about the most recent clothing item you purchased online.
2. How did you decide which size to order?
3. What information did you look for before adding it to the cart?
4. Have you ever received an item that looked or fit differently than expected?
5. What did you do when that happened?
6. Have you ever ordered more than one size of the same item? Why?
7. What would make you trust or distrust a virtual try-on preview?
8. Would you use a measurement-based avatar? What would stop you?
9. Would you upload a full-body photo for try-on? Under what conditions?
10. Would you prefer one realistic image, multiple angles, or a short video?
11. What would you expect a size recommendation to explain?
12. What data would you not want the product to store?

Ask about real past behaviour first. Avoid leading respondents toward saying they want AI.

## Sampling plan

- Target: 15–20 qualitative interviews initially.
- Include frequent and occasional online fashion shoppers.
- Include different clothing categories and size-confidence levels.
- Do not infer population-wide preferences from a small convenience sample.
- Obtain permission before recording or quoting participants.

## Research methods

### Interviews
20–30 minutes, semi-structured. Record only with permission.

### Survey
After initial interviews, write a short survey based on observed themes. Avoid treating survey responses as proof of actual purchase behaviour.

### Usability sessions
Test clickable prototypes of both avatar paths. Observe where users hesitate, misunderstand fit guidance, or distrust the result.

## Evidence log

For each finding, record:
- Finding ID
- Exact observation or participant quote (with permission)
- Participant context, anonymized
- Frequency in the research sample
- Confidence and limitations
- Product implication
- Follow-up question

## Synthesis

Group observations into themes:
- Size selection uncertainty
- Garment information quality
- Visual expectation mismatch
- Privacy and photo willingness
- Try-on usefulness
- Trust and explanation
- Shopping friction

## Decision gate

Proceed with photo-avatar MVP only if research supports user value and a privacy-safe, technically feasible implementation. Otherwise prioritize measurement avatar and fit intelligence.
